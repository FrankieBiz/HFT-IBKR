# Offline research methods

Implemented 2026-10-03. These are software research tools, not evidence of an investable edge. All execution remains synthetic through `quantlab.simulation.simulate`. No broker, credentials, network requests, model loading, deployment or activation exists in this subsystem.

## API and workflow

```python
from quantlab.research import evaluate
report = evaluate(bars, config, [(3, 12), (5, 20), (10, 40)], registry_path, provenance)
```

The pipeline accepts at least 120 chronological bars for exactly one symbol, and up to 32 candidate MA pairs. Each attempted candidate is registered before its configuration is constructed or simulated. Invalid candidates remain recorded as failed. Configurations, supplied provenance, a content hash of the bars, UTC creation times, outcomes and manual review notes are retained in local SQLite. Runs and event history are appended through the API; the database is not tamper-proof. An interrupted run remains `attempted`. Manual states are `pending_review`, `reviewed`, and `rejected`; none can activate trading.

Each expanding fold starts with the first half of observations as potential training data. Training intervals whose outcomes or two-observation gap touch the holdout are removed. Selection maximizes training-only net per-bar Sharpe, with lowest candidate index breaking ties. An undefined training Sharpe or invalid training simulation disqualifies that candidate for that fold. Full-history failures never influence earlier fold selection. Halted runs and invalid valuations are excluded from full-history diagnostics; invalid holdout folds make holdout PSR undefined. Valuation validity is read directly from every equity row. Holdouts are simulated from fresh cash and cold strategy history, with no inherited inventory or pending orders. The output includes each fold's selected parameters, original observation indices, returns and summary. These repeated fixed-capital experiments do not represent a single continuous capital account. Final inventory is marked, not forcibly liquidated.

The full-history candidate return matrix is a separate retrospective diagnostic input. Its columns identify successful original candidate indices. Failed candidate attempts prevent a DSR calculation; they are not silently excluded from its trial count. PBO may describe the successful subset, whose mapping is explicit. The registry defines a campaign and reports its cumulative attempted trial count. Reruns append records; DSR becomes undefined when earlier campaign trials exist because comparable historical Sharpe variance is not reconstructed. Duplicate candidate tuples count as attempts; identical trial scores can make DSR undefined. Unrecorded external searches remain unknown.

## Labels, purging, and CPCV

Triple barriers use close observations only. Entry and end indices are explicit, inclusive observation intervals. Upper/lower barriers must be positive; close observations cannot hit both simultaneously. A vertical expiry has label zero, even when its realized return is nonzero. Final shortened intervals are included, and the terminal close without a future observation is omitted. These are descriptive forward labels, not executable fills or predictions of an intrabar crossing.

CPCV partitions observations into contiguous groups, enumerates test-group combinations, purges overlapping training intervals, and removes training starts through the inclusive test-interval end plus embargo. Embargo units are observation indices, not elapsed time. This conservative implementation applies the extension to every test interval. Six groups with two test groups produce 15 splits. Empty training sets can occur and are exposed to callers. Enumeration is capped at 1,000 combinations.

`reconstruct_paths` accepts predictions indexed by split and observation. It places each group's successive test occurrence into a path, producing five complete paths for the six/two case. Every observation appears once per path and every split/group occurrence is used once. These are prediction paths; carrying portfolio state across independently fitted groups requires a separate execution model. The evaluation pipeline reports CPCV splits and does not fit CPCV models or fabricate CPCV trading paths. MA candidates are evaluated using expanding walk-forward instead. CPCV does not establish stationarity or eliminate researcher selection bias.

## Statistical definitions

Sharpe is mean excess return divided by sample standard deviation, without annualization; the risk-free return assumption is zero. At least three finite observations and nonzero variance are required. PSR uses the normal CDF of `(SR - benchmark) * sqrt((n - 1) / (1 - skew * SR + (kurtosis - 1) * SR² / 4))`. Skew and non-excess kurtosis use empirical central moments after normalizing returns by their maximum absolute magnitude, preventing overflow from large finite inputs. Nonfinite intermediate statistics and numerically unresolved variance fail closed. Barrier returns must also remain finite. DSR supplies a multiple-trial benchmark using trial Sharpe variance, explicit trial count, Euler's constant, and normal quantiles. For one trial its benchmark is zero; zero cross-trial variance with multiple trials is undefined. These asymptotic formulas do not adjust serial dependence. Source: [Bailey and López de Prado, The Deflated Sharpe Ratio](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf), checked 2026-10-03.

CSCV PBO splits the candidate return matrix into an even number of contiguous blocks (default eight), selecting half for training and the complement for testing. It selects the highest training Sharpe and ranks that candidate's test Sharpe. The relative rank is divided by candidate count plus one, transformed to log odds, and PBO counts nonpositive log odds. Training ties select the lowest original column; test ties receive average ranks. Zero variance in any required subset makes the entire statistic undefined. CSCV rearranges return observations and does not replay trading across blocks; PBO is separate from CPCV. Source: [Bailey et al., The Probability of Backtest Overfitting](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf), checked 2026-10-03.

In the pipeline DSR uses the highest-Sharpe full-history candidate’s own per-bar returns, cross-trial Sharpe variance from that identical full-history horizon, and all attempted trials. Ties select the lowest candidate index. The report includes the selected index, scope, observation count, start/end bar timestamps and trial count. Holdout PSR remains independent, and missing holdout folds do not invalidate an otherwise valid full-history DSR. Candidate correlation and repeated searches limit this approximation. No confidence threshold is used to grant approval. All undefined statistics return explicit reasons rather than NaN, infinity, or an apparent pass.
