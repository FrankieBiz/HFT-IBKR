# Order-book prediction: evidence and bounded experiment

Verified 2026-10-05. Offline research design only. Recommendation: establish causal queue-imbalance and order-flow-imbalance (OFI) baselines before comparing neural networks. Predictive accuracy is not executable profit. Hardware recommendations below are proposed limits for an RTX 3070 Ti with 8 GB VRAM and 32 GB RAM, not measurements on that machine.

## Primary-paper evidence

The linked versions identify what was reviewed. Methods/results text was inspected for nine papers; Stoikov and Kolm have explicitly limited access below. These are selected methodological references, not an exhaustive survey or a ranking by reported accuracy. Different labels, clocks and splits make headline scores incomparable.

1. **Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov, “The Price Impact of Order Book Events” (2014; reviewed 2011 manuscript).** Fifty US stocks, consolidated TAQ, April 2010. Linear OFI explains contemporaneous price changes over short clock-time intervals; depth helps explain its coefficient. This is principally an impact relationship, not evidence that the same-interval OFI can predict an already completed return. There is no equivalent chronological strategy holdout. Use depth-scaled, strictly lagged OFI as a candidate feature; reject its predictive interpretation if performance disappears when the target begins after feature completion. [Primary text, §§2–4](https://arxiv.org/html/1011.6402v3).

2. **Gould & Bonart, “Queue Imbalance as a One-Tick-Ahead Price Predictor” (2016).** Ten Nasdaq stocks, LOBSTER, 2014; logistic and local-logistic models predict the direction of the next mid-price change from bounded best-queue imbalance. The null ignores imbalance. Critically, the paper randomly splits aggregated observations 80/20 and samples uniformly inside intervals ending at the next price change. This supports association, not a chronological deployment result; the sampling schedule itself is retrospective. Re-test at predetermined observable events and on later days. Gains concentrated in large-tick names should not be generalized to every instrument. [Primary text, §§4–6](https://arxiv.org/html/1512.03492v1).

3. **Stoikov, “The Micro-Price: A High Frequency Estimator of Future Prices” (2018).** Defines a spread/imbalance-dependent adjustment as a limit of conditional expected mid-prices; compares against mid-price and weighted mid-price. It is a conditional price estimator, not a promised fill price. **Access limitation:** the author-deposited abstract and publication identity were verified; empirical dates, horizons, normalization and split were not independently checked in full text. Do not invent them. Retain as a later baseline only if a training-only transition estimate improves future-price error over weighted mid-price. [Author deposit](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2970694).

4. **Ntakaris et al., FI-2010 benchmark (2017 manuscript; 2018 publication).** Five Finnish Nasdaq Nordic stocks over ten trading days; released representations aggregate ten-event blocks. Labels compare current mid-price with future averaged prices; horizon units in the reviewed manuscript are representation steps, not seconds. Provides z-score, min–max and decimal scaling, ridge and single-hidden-layer baselines, and anchored day-forward testing. Audit the exact release's normalization provenance and horizon mapping. FI-2010 is useful for a pipeline reproduction; ten days of preprocessed data cannot establish durability, timestamp latency or fill realism. [Primary text, §§3–6](https://arxiv.org/html/1705.03233v2).

5. **Tsantekidis et al., “Using Deep Learning for Price Prediction by Exploiting Stationary Limit Order Book Features” (2018 manuscript).** Nasdaq Nordic, 1–14 June 2010; first seven days train, last three test. Tests SVM/MLP/CNN/LSTM and CNN–LSTM, comparing raw with engineered stationary inputs. Smoothed three-class labels use 10/50/100/200 event horizons and horizon-dependent thresholds. The paper discusses commission/slippage implications but does not establish realistic execution economics. A useful falsifier is whether stationary inputs retain their advantage under identical causal scaling and unsmoothed targets; gains from changing labels alone do not establish superior forecasting. [Primary text, §§4–6](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.09965v1).

6. **Zhang, Zohren & Roberts, DeepLOB (2019).** CNN/Inception/LSTM on FI-2010 and five LSE stocks during 2017. LSE uses chronological six/three/three-month train/validation/test periods, 100 past states, and normalization from the previous five days. Its LSE labels compare averaged future and past mid-prices; FI-2010 uses supplied labels. The trading illustration assumes mid-price execution and gross returns before fees. Compare against stationary-input models with the same labels; reject economic claims based on that illustration. An ablation with raw future price changes tests dependence on smoothing. [Primary text, §§III–V](https://arxiv.org/html/1808.03668v4).

7. **Sirignano & Cont, “Universal Features of Price Formation…” (2019; reviewed 2018 manuscript).** Approximately 1,000 Nasdaq stocks, 2014–2017; recurrent models predict the next price move in a price-change clock. Pooled models are compared with stock-specific linear/nonlinear models, including held-out stocks and later periods. Training through May 2015 is tested in June–August 2015 and January–March 2017. Standard financial normalizations did not improve their results. This motivates pooled-versus-specific comparison, not universal stationarity as an assumption. A held-out-stock or later-regime failure falsifies transfer for this project. The scale is unsuitable as an initial desktop reproduction. [Primary text, §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.06917v1).

8. **Kolm, Turiel & Westray, “Deep Order Flow Imbalance…” (2023).** Models 115 Nasdaq stocks and reports better prediction with stationary order-flow inputs than most raw-book models; effective forecast horizon is approximately two average price changes. That is not a fixed millisecond duration. **Access limitation:** publisher abstract/data statement verified; exact sample dates, transformations, split and horizon-specific labels require full-paper/code inspection before replication. Data are not redistributable and come from LOBSTER. Treat the result as motivation for a controlled raw-book-versus-flow ablation, not proof that deep models beat our logistic baseline or cover execution costs. [Publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/mafi.12413).

9. **Lucchese, Pakkanen & Veraart, “The Short-Term Predictability of Returns…” (2024; reviewed manuscript v3).** Ten stocks; eleven five-week windows in January 2019–January 2020. Fifth week tests; validation days are randomly selected within the preceding four weeks. Compares raw-book, flow and volume representations using model confidence sets. Nine event horizons span 10–1,000 updates. Targets smooth around the future endpoint by ±5 events, so label availability extends beyond the nominal horizon. Uses five-day scaling for book/flow and input-window max scaling for volume. Immediate book access and non-tradable mid-prices limit economic interpretation. Reproduce representation gains using chronological validation and full label-end purging. [Primary text, §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.13777v3).

10. **Briola, Bartolucci & Aste, “Deep Limit Order Book Forecasting: A Microstructural Guide” (2024 manuscript; 2025 publication).** Fifteen Nasdaq stocks, 2017–2019; experiments use 45 training, five interspersed validation and ten subsequent test days per year. Five-day rolling normalization; unsmoothed mid-price differences with one-tick thresholds at 10/50/100 updates. LOBFrame supports reproducibility and analysis by tick regime. This is a useful corrective to FI-2010-only evaluation. Confidence filtering can reduce coverage sharply; report retained observations, not just conditional accuracy. Reject a result driven by one tick regime or a tiny selected subset. [Primary text, §§4–7](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.09267v4).

11. **Zhong, Lin & Yang, “Representation Learning of Limit Order Book: A Comprehensive Study and Benchmarking” (2025 preprint).** LOBench uses five SZSE stocks from 2019, sampled every three seconds. Compares nine neural architectures, including CNN, LSTM and transformers; five future snapshots define a smoothed target. Snapshot-level joint price/volume normalization preserves structural relationships. Each day's earliest 80% trains and latest 20% tests: this is not a calendar-forward deployment split across the year. Its value is representation/normalization benchmarking, not direct evidence about Nasdaq event-time alpha. Re-run across disjoint calendar periods before using model rankings. [Primary text, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02139v1).

## Experiment recommendation — proposed, not performed

**Priority:** CPU ridge regression for executable returns, with logistic imbalance as later scientific reproduction → small stationary-flow MLP/LSTM → DeepLOB-style CNN/LSTM → transformer only after demonstrated incremental gain. This order reflects auditability and bounded compute, not a universal performance ranking. For later neural trials, propose sequence length 100, batch 32 and at most one million parameters; measure peak memory before scaling. Stream day shards rather than materializing overlapping windows. No measured throughput or fit guarantee is available for the proposed hardware.

1. **Freeze scope:** SPY, long/cash, aggressive execution; retain the daily strategy as a separate control. Propose at least 120 scheduled sessions plus two boundary-gap sessions: 60 training, 30 validation and 30 sealed testing. These are illustrative pilot minima, not proof of durability. Use only an authorized timestamped dataset; no acquisition, subscription or broker connection is authorized by this note. FI-2010 alone supports benchmark reproduction, not this experiment.
2. **Specify provenance:** distinguish a single exchange book from consolidated best quotes. Consolidated L1 bid/ask sizes are aggregated displayed liquidity, not queue position. A best-quote change can reflect a venue change; derived OFI is a consolidated quote-flow proxy, not a direct replication of exchange-specific LOBSTER evidence. Document feed coverage, aggregation, timestamp/sequence semantics and gaps before interpreting results.
3. **Enforce causality:** features use only events received by the decision time; preserve exchange and receive timestamps separately. Resolve equal timestamps with source sequence or a documented collapse rule. Reject stale/crossed quotes and invalid segments; reset across sessions, halts and corporate-action discontinuities. Missing receive timestamps prevents validating real observation latency.
4. **Fix six candidates:** two feature sets—best-size imbalance, spread and trailing one-second return; those features plus trailing one-second normalized OFI—crossed with 1/10/60-second horizons. Use ridge with fixed regularization, training-only standardization, and a preregistered OFI denominator. Compute targets as fixed-size entry-ask to exit-bid price changes after prescribed latency and costs. Define policy horizons from locally received terminal entry evidence (including partial-fill accounting), and freeze quote selection, size, fees, latency and action threshold before testing. A displayed quote cannot guarantee executable size or a fill.
5. **Separate selection from evaluation:** six candidates constitute a search; select one by a preregistered validation criterion and expose the sealed test once. Purge all labels whose actual execution/exit timestamps cross partition boundaries; discard boundary input windows and overnight labels. Record all candidates. Report forecast errors against zero/training-mean controls, daily net results, turnover, drawdown and paired day-block uncertainty. Daily control comparisons must use common test dates and capital assumptions. Sensitivity axes are stress tests, not extra opportunities to choose a winner.
6. **Use falsifiers:** stop expansion if OFI adds no out-of-sample improvement over the smaller feature set, if gains vanish after spread/costs, or if required latency is below the achievable budget. Report 1/10/60-second failures separately rather than hiding them behind event-time accuracy. Permit one small flow network with three fixed seeds only after baseline success and a fresh evaluation design. Thirty sealed days remain preliminary. Passive fills, market making, broker connectivity and live orders are outside this experiment.
